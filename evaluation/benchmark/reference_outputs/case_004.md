# **Exhaustive Operational Protocol for Cpf1-Mediated Knockin at the AAVS1 Locus in Human Pluripotent Stem Cells and HEK293T Cells**

1. Reagent, Buffer, and Culture Media Preparation 1.1. Prepare a 0.2% (w/v) gelatin solution by dissolving 0.8 g of gelatin in 400 mL of molecular biology grade water (MilliQ), followed by sterilization via autoclaving at 121 °C for 15 minutes, and store at 4 °C for plate coating1. 1.2. Formulate a 10X PBS stock solution by dissolving 25.6 g of Na2HPO4·7H2O, 13.56 g of Na2HPO4, 80 g of NaCl, 2 g of KCl, and 2 g of KH2PO4 in 1,000 mL of MilliQ water, adjust the pH to 7.4, and sterilize by autoclaving for 15 minutes at 121 °C2. 1.3. Synthesize a Ca2+ and Mg2+ solution by adding 5 g of CaCl2 and 5 g of MgCl2·6H2O into 500 mL of water, filter the solution through a 0.22 μm filter, and sterilize in an autoclave on a 45-minute liquid cycle2. 1.4. Combine 990 mL of 1X PBS (diluted from the 10X stock) with 10 mL of the Ca2+ and Mg2+ solution to create PBS+, magnetically stir for 30 minutes until transparent, filter through a 0.22 μm filter, and store at 4 °C2. 1.5. Formulate the DNA extraction buffer by combining 25 mL of 1 M Tris (pH 8.0) for a 50 mM final concentration, 10 mL of 0.5 M EDTA (pH 8.0) for 10 mM, 10 mL of 5 M NaCl for 100 mM, 25 mL of 10% SDS for 0.5%, and 430 mL of MilliQ water; add 25 μL of Proteinase K (20 mg/mL) per 1 mL of buffer immediately prior to use2. 1.6. Prepare the complete culture medium for HEK293T cells (and mouse embryonic fibroblasts, MEFs) by integrating the components detailed in the corresponding table, vacuum filter through a 0.22 μm membrane, and store at 4 °C2. 1.7. Prepare the culture medium for human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs) by combining the reagents specified in the media formulation table, filter under sterile conditions, and keep protected from light at 4 °C for a maximum of two weeks2. 1.8. Formulate the 50X TAE buffer for electrophoresis by dissolving 121 g of Tris base (2 M final), 50 mL of 0.5 M EDTA at pH 8.0 (50 mM final), and 28.55 mL of glacial acetic acid (1 M final) in MilliQ water up to a total volume of 500 mL2. 1.9. Reconstitute the validated lyophilized crRNA targeting the AAVS1 locus (PPP1R12C intron 1\) in nuclease-free water or 1X TE buffer (RNase/DNase-free) to obtain a final stock concentration of 100 μM (100 pmol/μL)3. 1.10. Aliquot the crRNA solution into RNase-free microcentrifuge tubes in volumes of 5 μL to 10 μL per tube to prevent repeated freeze-thaw cycles, and store immediately at \-80 °C4.

2. Extracellular Matrix Preparation and Plate Coating 2.1. Thaw a vial of Matrigel on ice overnight at 4 °C to prevent premature temperature-induced polymerization6. 2.2. Dilute the Matrigel in cold DMEM/F-12 medium by strictly following the lot-specific dilution factor provided by the manufacturer, using serological pipettes and pipette tips pre-chilled to 4 °C7. 2.3. Dispense a volume of 150 μL to 200 μL of the diluted Matrigel solution per cm² of surface area into the culture plates intended for hPSCs (e.g., approximately 1 mL per well in a 6-well plate)7. 2.4. Incubate the Matrigel-coated culture vessels at 37 °C for at least 30 minutes to allow complete gel formation and polymerization7. 2.5. Store unused coated plates at 4 °C sealed with Parafilm, containing the Matrigel coating solution, for a maximum period of one week prior to cell seeding7.  
3. Culture and Maintenance of HEK293T Cells 3.1. Remove a cryovial of HEK293T cells from liquid nitrogen storage and rapidly thaw it in a 37 °C water bath until only a microscopic ice crystal remains7. 3.2. Gently transfer the cell suspension to a 15 mL conical tube in a laminar flow hood and add 5 mL of pre-warmed complete HEK293T medium dropwise while gently swirling the tube to minimize osmotic shock7. 3.3. Centrifuge the HEK293T cell suspension at 500 x g for 5 minutes at room temperature to pellet the cells8. 3.4. Aspirate the supernatant containing the cryoprotective agent (DMSO) and carefully resuspend the cell pellet in 10 mL of fresh complete HEK293T medium1. 3.5. Seed the cell suspension into a 10 cm culture dish or a T-75 flask and place the vessel in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with a 5% CO2 concentration4. 3.6. Replace the culture medium every 2 to 3 days and subculture the cells via trypsinization (Trypsin-EDTA at room temperature) when they reach 80-90% confluency4. 3.7. Avoid using HEK293T cells for electroporation that have exceeded passage number 20, as prolonged passaging drastically reduces cell viability following nucleofection.  
4. Culture and Maintenance of Human Pluripotent Stem Cells (hPSCs) 4.1. Thaw a cryovial of low-passage hPSCs (fewer than 20 passages) in a 37 °C water bath and transfer the cells to a 15 mL conical tube7. 4.2. Dropwise add 4 mL of mTeSR Plus medium (or the hPSC medium prepared in step 1.7) supplemented with ROCK enzyme inhibitor (Y-27632) to a final concentration of 10 μM, gently swirling the tube6. 4.3. Centrifuge the cell suspension at 250 x g for 3 minutes, aspirate the supernatant, and resuspend the pellet in 1 mL of fresh medium supplemented with 10 μM ROCK inhibitor7. 4.4. Transfer the hPSC suspension to the previously prepared Matrigel-coated plate and incubate at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO27. 4.5. Replace the medium daily with 1.5 mL of fresh medium without the addition of ROCK inhibitor7. 4.6. Subculture the cells when they reach approximately 75-80% confluency (generally 3 to 4 days post-seeding) using 1 mL of 0.5 mM EDTA or Accutase per well of a 6-well plate, incubating at 37 °C for 3 to 8 minutes6. 4.7. Add fresh mTeSR Plus medium supplemented with 1% Revitacell supplement or 10 μM ROCK inhibitor exactly 1 hour before initiating the detachment procedures for nucleofection to optimize single-cell survival13.  
5. Preparation and Quality Control of the Donor Vector and CRISPR-Cpf1 Complex 5.1. Design the homology-directed repair (HDR) donor vector to contain 5' and 3' homology arms specific to the AAVS1 locus, flanking a gene of interest (GOI) expression cassette, a constitutive promoter (e.g., CAG or CMV), a selection marker (e.g., Puromycin, Neomycin), and a T2A self-cleaving peptide or fluorescent marker (e.g., EGFP, mCherry)15. 5.2. Purify the plasmid donor vector from bacterial cultures using an endotoxin-free maxi-prep kit, ensuring the wash buffer is critically dried by centrifugation (≥12,000 x g for 2-3 min) before elution7. 5.3. Quantify the purified donor plasmid using a microvolume spectrophotometer (Nanodrop), verifying that the concentration is greater than 1 μg/μL and that the A260/A280 purity ratio is strictly between 1.8 and 2.07. 5.4. Select a crRNA that specifically recognizes the thymine-rich (5'-TTTV-3') protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) characteristic of Cas12a (Cpf1) nucleases derived from Acidaminococcus sp. (AsCpf1) or Lachnospiraceae bacterium (LbCpf1) within intron 1 of the PPP1R12C (AAVS1) gene19. 5.5. Confirm via bioinformatics alignment tools that the validated crRNA lacks significant off-target homologies in the human genome (GRCh38/hg38)1.  
6. In Vitro Assembly of the Cpf1-crRNA Ribonucleoprotein (RNP) Complex 6.1. Thaw the reconstituted, lyophilized crRNA (100 μM) and the Cas12a/Cpf1 nuclease protein (100 μM) on ice immediately prior to the assembly process3. 6.2. Prepare the specific resuspension buffer for the electroporation system: use Resuspension Buffer R for the Thermo Fisher Neon system, or Primary Cell Solution P3 / SF Cell Line Solution for the Lonza 4D-Nucleofector system9. 6.3. Transfer 7.0 μL of the selected electroporation buffer into a sterile, nuclease-free 0.2 mL PCR tube9. 6.4. Add 5.0 μL of 500 μM or 100 μM crRNA (depending on the desired molar optimization, typically requiring 500 pmol) to the buffer solution in the PCR tube10. 6.5. Add 2.5 μL of 100 μM Cas12a (Cpf1) nuclease (250 pmol) to the buffer and crRNA mixture to establish an optimal protein-to-crRNA molar ratio that maximizes cleavage efficiency, totaling a reaction volume of 14.5 μL10. 6.6. Gently mix the solution by pipetting up and down 5 times, meticulously avoiding the introduction of air bubbles that could compromise enzymatic stability4. 6.7. Incubate the Cpf1-crRNA RNP complex at room temperature (20 °C – 25 °C) for exactly 20 minutes to allow covalent binding and structural stabilization of the ribonucleoproteins8. 6.8. Keep the formed RNP complex protected from direct light exposure until the exact moment of its addition to the target cell suspension3.

7. Harvesting, Dissociation, and Counting of Target Cells 7.1. Aspirate the culture medium from the HEK293T flasks (at 60-80% confluency) and wash the cell monolayer once with 5 mL of 1X PBS (without Ca2+ or Mg2+) to remove serum trypsin inhibitors1. 7.2. Add 2 mL of room temperature Trypsin-EDTA solution to the HEK293T flask and incubate at 37 °C for 3 to 5 minutes, monitoring detachment under the microscope1. 7.3. Neutralize the enzymatic reaction by adding 8 mL of serum-supplemented complete HEK293T culture medium and pipette vigorously to dissociate cell aggregates into a single-cell suspension1. 7.4. Aspirate the culture medium from the hPSC plates and wash the cell monolayer by gently adding 2 mL of 1X PBS (without Ca2+ or Mg2+) per well13. 7.5. Add 0.5 mL of Accutase per well to the hPSCs and incubate at 37 °C for a maximum of 10 minutes (8 minutes being optimal) until the cells acquire a spherical morphology and detach13. 7.6. Add 2 mL of DMEM/F12 medium to the hPSC wells to neutralize the Accutase, gently wash the cells off the plate using a p1000 pipette, and transfer the suspension to a 15 mL conical tube. 7.7. Extract a 10 μL aliquot of the cell suspension (both HEK293T and hPSC) and mix it with 10 μL of 0.4% Trypan blue solution1. 7.8. Load 10 μL of the cell and Trypan blue mixture into a Neubauer hemocytometer and perform a count of viable cells (unstained) and dead cells (stained blue) under an optical microscope1. 7.9. Calculate the total cell concentration and confirm that the viability strictly exceeds the 90% threshold, a fundamental requirement for electroporation success; discard suspensions with lower viability to avoid disproportionate post-transfection toxicity3.  
8. Setup and Execution of Electroporation: Lonza 4D-Nucleofector System (Method 1\) 8.1. Calculate and transfer the volume of cell suspension containing exactly between 1.0 x 10^5 and 2.0 x 10^5 viable cells for each planned electroporation reaction into a sterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube1. 8.2. Centrifuge the HEK293T cell aliquots at 500 x g for 5 minutes at room temperature1. 8.3. Centrifuge the hPSC aliquots at 200 x g for 7 minutes at 4 °C (using an acceleration of 9 and deceleration of 5 in the centrifuge settings). 8.4. Meticulously aspirate the entirety of the supernatant without mechanically disrupting the cell pellet8. 8.5. Resuspend the cell pellet precisely in 10.5 μL of nucleofector solution (using SF Cell Line Solution for HEK293T or Primary Cell Solution P3 for hPSCs)11. 8.6. Add the 10.5 μL of cell suspension to the PCR tube containing the 14.5 μL of pre-incubated Cpf1-crRNA RNP complex8. 8.7. Introduce 1.0 μg to 2.0 μg of the endotoxin-free AAVS1 donor plasmid and, optionally, 1 μL of Alt-R Cas9/12a Electroporation Enhancer into the mixture13. 8.8. Mix the final suspension of approximately 26 μL by pipetting 2 to 3 times, ensuring the complete absence of air bubbles. 8.9. Transfer 20 μL of the nucleofection reaction mixture into the corresponding wells of a 16-well Nucleocuvette strip. 8.10. Gently tap the Nucleocuvette strip against the surface of the biosafety cabinet to expel any residual air bubbles at the bottom of the cuvette. 8.11. Turn on the Lonza 4D-Nucleofector system, navigate the X-Unit interface, and select the pre-optimized CM-130 program for HEK293T cells, or the CA-137 program for hPSCs. 8.12. Insert the Nucleocuvette strip into the system's carrier and press the START command on the touchscreen to execute the electrical pulse. 8.13. Remove the Nucleocuvette strip immediately after pulse completion and incubate it at absolute rest at 37 °C for 10 to 20 minutes to facilitate the structural reorganization of the cellular lipid bilayer. 8.14. Add 20 μL of pre-warmed recovery medium directly to each well of the Nucleocuvette and gently pipette to resuspend the edited cells.  
9. Setup and Execution of Electroporation: Neon Transfection System (Method 2\) 9.1. Follow the cell pellet preparation and centrifugation steps described in the previous subsection (8.1 to 8.4) to obtain 2.0 x 10^5 cells per reaction. 9.2. Resuspend the cell pellet in 10.5 μL of Resuspension Buffer R provided with the Neon transfection kit10. 9.3. Integrate the 10.5 μL of resuspended cells with the 14.5 μL of Cpf1-crRNA RNP complex and the AAVS1 donor vector (1-2 μg)9. 9.4. Fill a sterile Neon tube with 3 mL of Electrolytic Buffer E and insert it into the Neon pipette station until a mechanical click is heard, confirming the firm connection of the side electrode. 9.5. Press the top button of the Neon pipette to the second stop to open the clamp, insert the top head into a 10 μL Neon tip, and slowly release the button while applying downward pressure to seal. 9.6. Press the button to the first stop, submerge the Neon tip into the cell/RNP/donor mixture, and slowly release the button to aspirate exactly 10 μL of the sample10. 9.7. Visually inspect the loaded tip to ensure no air has entered; discard the sample and aspirate a new one if air bubbles are observed, as they can cause lethal electrical arcs during electroporation. 9.8. Insert the Neon pipette vertically into the tube of the pipette station. 9.9. Configure the electroporation parameters on the Neon device interface: for HEK293T use 1700 V, 20 ms pulse width, 1 pulse; for hPSCs use 1200 V, 20 ms pulse width, 2 pulses8. 9.10. Execute the pulse by pressing the screen, wait for the system to display 'Complete', remove the Neon pipette, and immediately inject the 10 μL of transfected volume into the prepared recovery well10.

10. Post-Electroporation Care and Recovery 10.1. Prepare a 24-well culture plate by adding 500 μL of complete HEK293T medium per well for the HEK293T cells and incubate at 37 °C prior to electroporation1. 10.2. Prepare a Matrigel-coated 24-well plate by adding 500 μL of mTeSR Plus medium supplemented with ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 (10 μM) or CloneR supplement per well for the hPSCs, and maintain at 37 °C. 10.3. Carefully transfer the electroporated cell suspension from the Nucleocuvette or Neon tip directly into the 500 μL of pre-warmed medium9. 10.4. Agitate the 24-well plate with a two-dimensional figure-eight motion to ensure homogeneous cell distribution across the well surface. 10.5. Incubate the plates in the humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for a critical period of 48 to 72 hours to allow repair of voltage-induced cellular damage and genomic expression1. 10.6. Change the medium for the hPSCs 24 hours post-nucleofection to fresh mTeSR Plus medium without the Y-27632 ROCK inhibitor, or partially replace the CloneR medium at 48 hours to compensate for evaporation and chemical degradation7. 10.7. Change the complete medium for the HEK293T cells at 48 hours if significant accumulation of floating cellular debris induced by post-pulse cell death is detected.  
11. Pharmacological Selection and Enrichment of Integrated Cells 11.1. Identify the antibiotic resistance gene encoded in the AAVS1 donor vector cassette (typically Puromycin, Neomycin, or Zeocin resistance, under the control of promoters such as PGK or SV40)15. 11.2. Establish the optimal lethal antibiotic concentration via a dose-response curve (kill curve) previously performed on non-transfected cells (for example, for Puromycin, doses usually range between 0.5 and 1.5 μg/mL)15. 11.3. Aspirate the recovery medium from the electroporated wells 72 hours post-transfection and replace it with 500 μL of the prepared antibiotic selection medium25. 11.4. Renew the selection medium daily for the hPSCs and every 48 hours for the HEK293T cells, maintaining a strict negative control (non-electroporated cells exposed to the antibiotic)6. 11.5. Continue the selective treatment for a continuous period of 5 to 7 days, or until 100% of the cells in the negative control plate have undergone apoptosis and total detachment29. 11.6. Halt the pharmacological selection and expand the bulk pool of surviving cells in standard culture medium to recover population density30.

12. Clonal Isolation via Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS) 12.1. Detach the enriched cell population using Trypsin-EDTA (HEK293T) or 0.5 mM EDTA / Accutase (hPSCs) and neutralize the enzymatic reaction1. 12.2. Centrifuge the cell suspension, aspirate the medium, and wash the pellet twice by resuspending it in sterile 1X PBS buffer supplemented with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) or 2% serum to maintain viability2. 12.3. Filter the cell suspension through a sterile 40 μm cell strainer attached to a polystyrene tube to eradicate cell clumps that may clog the sorter nozzle. 12.4. Load the sample into a high-speed cell sorter (e.g., BD FACSAria III) configured with the lasers and optical filters corresponding to the fluorophores co-expressed in the AAVS1 donor vector (such as EGFP, tdTomato, or mCherry)1. 12.5. Adjust the gating windows excluding cell doublets (plotting FSC-H vs. FSC-A) and isolating the top 5-10% of the population exhibiting the highest target fluorescence intensity, compared to a non-fluorescent negative control27. 12.6. Sort individual cells (single-cell sort mode) directly into the wells of a 96-well plate pre-coated with Matrigel, containing 100 μL of conditioned medium or medium enriched with ROCK inhibitor (10 μM) per well to promote single-cell survival27.  
13. Expansion and Maintenance of Edited Individual Clones 13.1. Maintain the 96-well plates inoculated with single cells in a strictly controlled environment within the incubator at 37 °C for 7 consecutive days without physically moving them to prevent disruption of cell anchorage31. 13.2. Implement partial culture medium changes (extraction of 50 μL and addition of 50 μL of fresh medium) every 48 hours starting on the seventh day of incubation7. 13.3. Inspect the plate bed under a phase-contrast microscope between day 10 and day 14 post-sorting to positively identify those wells containing a single healthy and morphologically coherent cell colony31. 13.4. Aspirate the total volume of medium from the wells housing validated colonies and wash once with 100 μL of 1X PBS. 13.5. Introduce 30 μL of dissociation agent (Trypsin-EDTA or Accutase) and incubate at 37 °C until focal detachment of the colony is observed1. 13.6. Neutralize by adding 70 μL of culture medium and triturate the colony by pipetting up/down to form a single-cell solution1. 13.7. Divide the 100 μL suspension equally into two parallel 96-well plates: designate one master plate for continuous propagation of the cell lineage and designate the second plate exclusively for destructive genetic assays28.  
14. Genomic DNA Extraction for Genotyping 14.1. Allow cells to proliferate in the 96-well genotyping plate until cell coverage reaches between 80% and 100% confluency. 14.2. Completely remove the culture medium by aspiration and wash the residual cell monolayer twice using 100 μL of 1X PBS per well1. 14.3. Dispense exactly 50 μL of a commercial rapid lysis solution (e.g., QuickExtract DNA Extraction Solution) or the extraction buffer formulated in step 1.5 into each well1. 14.4. Seal the genotyping plate with an adhesive film and subject it to orbital shaking on a microplate shaker at 300 rpm for 5 minutes at room temperature1. 14.5. Transfer the 50 μL of crude lysate into sterile PCR tube strips1. 14.6. Place the PCR strips into a thermal cycler and execute the following thermal profile for DNA extraction and protease denaturation: 65 °C held for 15 minutes, followed by an increase to 95 °C for 15 minutes, and a drop to 4 °C for short-term storage1. 14.7. Quantify the yield and purity of the extracted genomic DNA by depositing 1-2 μL of the sample into a spectrophotometer, confirming a minimum operational genomic yield of 10 ng/μL7.  
15. Verification of Targeted Integration via Junction PCR 15.1. Design a forward primer that specifically hybridizes to the endogenous genomic sequence upstream of the 5' homology arm of the AAVS1 locus, paired with a reverse primer complementary to an exclusive internal region of the exogenous donor vector cassette10. 15.2. Design a second set composed of a forward primer hybridized to the 3' end of the exogenous donor insert paired with a reverse primer complementary to the endogenous genomic sequence downstream of the 3' homology arm of AAVS125. 15.3. Prepare a 25 μL PCR master mix consolidating 50 ng of template genomic DNA, 0.5 μM of each primer pair, 200 μM of dNTPs, and 1 Unit of High-Fidelity DNA Polymerase in a compatible buffer18. 15.4. Subject the mixture to the thermal amplification program: initial denaturation at 98 °C for 30 seconds; 35 iterative cycles of 98 °C for 10 seconds, primer-specific annealing temperature for 20 seconds, and elongation at 72 °C for 60 seconds per target kilobase; culminating with a stabilizing final extension at 72 °C for 5 minutes31. 15.5. Load 5 μL of each resulting PCR amplicon onto a 2% agarose gel infused with ethidium bromide or another intercalating agent, applying an electrical field of 100 volts for approximately 45 minutes29. 15.6. Evaluate the migratory band pattern under ultraviolet irradiation (UV transilluminator) to evidence the presence of amplicons of predetermined size that physically corroborate the correct homologous recombination on both flanks of the AAVS1 (PPP1R12C) gene31.

16. Analysis of Off-Target Effects via T7E1 Endonuclease Assay 16.1. Identify in silico the host genome sequences with the highest probability of undergoing erroneous cuts (off-target sites) based on homology with the crRNA spacer, and PCR-amplify fragments of 400-600 base pairs that flank these candidate regions1. 16.2. Purify the resulting amplicons using a silica column kit for the elimination of free nucleotides and elute the target DNA in 30 μL of nuclease-free water2. 16.3. Assemble a 20 μL thermodynamic heteroduplex formation reaction encompassing 200 ng of the purified PCR amplicon and 1X NEB Buffer 2 reaction buffer18. 16.4. Subject the mixture to the controlled renaturation thermal profile: heat to 95 °C for 5 minutes to dissociate the strands; induce a rapid thermal drop to 85 °C at a rate of \-2 °C/second; continue with a gradual thermal drop to 25 °C at a rate of \-0.1 °C/second; and set the resting temperature to 4 °C18. 16.5. Add 5 Units (typically 0.5 μL) of T7 phage Endonuclease I (T7E1) enzyme to the heteroduplex reaction tube and incubate the enzymatic system strictly at 37 °C for 60 minutes10. 16.6. Subject the T7E1 digested products to electrophoresis on a 2% agarose gel to elucidate the presence of cleaved fragments, which would reveal unintended editing by the Cas12a system at aberrant genomic positions18.  
17. Genomic Sequence Confirmation via Sanger Sequencing 17.1. Retrieve the junction PCR amplicons generated in section 15 that demonstrated band sizes indicative of accurate knockin integration into the target genome. 17.2. Purify these amplicons from the agarose gel using a gel extraction kit or a microcolumn-based PCR purification kit (e.g., QIAquick), proceeding to quantify the eluted nucleic acid2. 17.3. Prepare individual Sanger sequencing reactions using 20 to 50 ng of the purified DNA amplicon combined with specific directional primers that flank the AAVS1 homologous recombination site2. 17.4. Analyze the resulting chromatograms supplied by the capillary sequencer using bioinformatic alignment software, scrupulously verifying the molecular junctions at the crossings of the 5' and 3' homology arms to ensure that splicing was achieved without incurring insertions, deletions, or point mutations caused by anomalous end-joining events2.  
18. Long-Term Cryopreservation of Validated Edited Clones 18.1. Return to the master expansion plate described in step 13.7 and mobilize the genotypically validated clones to larger capacity culture vessels (scaling sequentially from 24-well plates to 6-well plates and subsequently to T-75 flasks)7. 18.2. Monitor the status of the scaled cultures and harvest the hPSC clones using 0.5 mM EDTA precisely when they reach 80% spatial confluency7. 18.3. Resuspend the hPSC cell pellet in 1 mL of Freezing Medium I tempered to room temperature (20 °C – 25 °C), formulated to mitigate the initial osmotic shock7. 18.4. Distribute 500 μL aliquots of the protective cell suspension into sterile cryovials with an O-ring and two-dimensional barcode7. 18.5. Gradually instill, dropwise, 500 μL of Freezing Medium II, pre-chilled to 4 °C and highly concentrated in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), into each cryovial while applying lateral tapping to guarantee complete homogeneity of the cryoprotectant7. 18.6. In the parallel case of the HEK293T clones, detach them using Trypsin-EDTA, apply centrifugation, and resuspend the resulting pellet directly in 1 mL of standard HEK293T freezing medium composed of DMEM enriched with 20% Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS) and 10% DMSO as a cryogenic stabilizer7. 18.7. Hermetically seal the cryovials and rotate them via a simple inversion to finalize the mixing7. 18.8. Immediately house the prepared cryovials in the core of an isopropanol-based controlled-rate freezing container (e.g., "Mr. Frosty") and insert it into a \-80 °C mechanical freezer for a window of 14 to 18 hours to guarantee a thermally balanced cellular cryogenic descent at a steadfast rate of \-1 °C per minute7. 18.9. Conclude the long-term preservation process by transferring the cryovials of the edited knockin hPSCs and HEK293T from the \-80 °C freezer to the vapor (or liquid) phase compartments of a liquid nitrogen immersion tank, ensuring the survival of the transgenic cellular phenotype indefinitely7.
