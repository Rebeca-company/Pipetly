# Protocol for Primary Pituitary Adenoma Cell Culture on Matrigel-Coated Microporous Membrane

**Note:** This protocol derived from the source text provides a method for primary pituitary adenoma cell culture using a microporous membrane system. The source text does **NOT** describe alginate bead encapsulation for long-term culture. Therefore, steps for alginate bead encapsulation are not included.

## 1. Materials Preparation

1.  Prepare membrane inserts:
    -   Use Falcon 3091 or 3092 cell-culture inserts (3.0-μm pore-size) for a six-well plate (Falcon 3502).
2.  Prepare Matrigel coating solution:
    -   Thaw Matrigel at 4°C overnight and keep on ice.
    -   Dilute Matrigel 1:8 in cold distilled water at 4°C or on ice, and vortex until completely dissolved.
3.  Prepare complete medium (Coon's Modified Ham's F-12):
    -   Dissolve Coon's Modified Ham's F-12 powder in distilled water.
    -   Supplement with: 10% (or 15%) fetal bovine serum, 0.2% glucose, 100 U/mL penicillin, 100 μg/mL streptomycin, 10 mM HEPES, 14 mM NaHCO₃.
    -   Buffer to pH 7.1–7.3.
    -   Store at 4°C; renew after 1 month. Incubate at 37°C when in use.
4.  Prepare Tris buffer for red blood cell lysis:
    -   **Solution A:** 0.83% NH₄Cl solution.
    -   **Solution B:** Dissolve 20.6 g Tris in ~700 mL distilled water, adjust pH to 7.65 with 1N HCl, bring volume to 1 L with distilled water.
    -   Mix Solutions A and B at a 9:1 ratio.
    -   Sterilize by filtering through a 0.45-μm filter.
    -   Store at 4°C for up to 3 months.
5.  Prepare 0.25% trypsin solution (0.25% trypsin in Hank's balanced salt solution).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1. Coating of Membrane Inserts with Matrigel (Perform 1–2 days before use)
1.  Add 400 μL of the diluted Matrigel solution to the inside of each membrane insert in a six-well plate.
2.  Gently shake the plate to evenly coat the membrane surface.
3.  Air-dry overnight in a laminar flow hood with the cover slightly ajar.
4.  Store the coated inserts sterile in the six-well plate at room temperature until use.

### 2.2. Sampling and Transport of Pituitary Adenoma Tissue
1.  Provide the clinical collaborator a labeled 50-mL sterile centrifuge tube containing ~15 mL of complete medium.
2.  If culture is delayed, provide an ice pack to keep tissue at 0–4°C; ensure transport and culture occur within 6 hours.
3.  At the surgical site, place adenoma tissue in sterile physiological saline in a sterile plastic dish and wash once to remove contaminated blood.
4.  Transfer several tissue pieces to the medium in the transport container and deliver to the culture lab immediately.

### 2.3. Primary Culture on Microporous Membrane
1.  Transfer all adenoma tissue to a 60-mm Petri dish and mince/crush using scalpels.
2.  Add adequate 0.25% trypsin solution to suspend the crushed tissue; pipet several times.
3.  Incubate at 37°C for 20 minutes, pipetting several times during incubation.
4.  Transfer the digest to a new 50-mL centrifuge tube and pipet rapidly ~10 times to disperse tissue into a cell suspension containing small aggregates.
5.  Filter the cell suspension through a cell strainer (Falcon 2350) into a new 50-mL centrifuge tube to obtain a single-cell suspension.
6.  Press all remaining pieces against the strainer mesh with a cotton swab or pipet tip and rinse with complete medium.
7.  Centrifuge the filtered suspension at 1500 rpm for 10 minutes to pellet cells.
8.  Resuspend pellet in 5 mL serum-free medium, transfer to a 15-mL tube, centrifuge at 1000 rpm for 5 minutes, and repeat this wash step once.
9.  Add 2–3 mL of Tris buffer (NH₄Cl) to the final pellet to lyse contaminating red cells; resuspend and incubate at room temperature for 2–5 minutes (solution turns light red wine color).
10. Centrifuge at 1000 rpm for 5 minutes.
11. Resuspend pellet in 5 mL serum-free medium, centrifuge at 1000 rpm for 5 minutes, and repeat to thoroughly rinse Tris buffer.
12. Resuspend pellet in adequate complete medium to achieve a cell density of 1–5 × 10⁵ cells/mL (typically ~5 mL medium).
13. Seed ~1 mL of the cell suspension into each coated membrane insert. The insert is placed in a six-well plate pre-moistened with ~0.5 mL complete medium in the well.
14. Incubate at 37°C in 5% CO₂ / 95% air.
15. The medium in the insert will gradually pass through the membrane during incubation (observed within several hours).
16. Change the medium in the well every 3–4 days by transferring the insert to a new well containing ~1.5 mL fresh complete medium. Do **not** add medium to the inside of the membrane insert.
17. Maintain culture until confluence.

### 2.4. Subculture on Microporous Membrane (Upon Reaching Confluence)
1.  Replace the medium in the well with ~1.5 mL of 0.25% trypsin solution and add ~1.0 mL trypsin solution to the inside of the membrane insert.
2.  Incubate at 37°C for 5–10 minutes, or until cells loosen from the membrane.
3.  Add ~1.0 mL complete medium to the inside of the insert and pipet up and down to free all cells and obtain a single-cell suspension.
4.  Transfer to a 15-mL centrifuge tube and centrifuge at 1500 rpm for 10 minutes. Resuspend pellet in serum-free medium and centrifuge again at 1000 rpm for 5 minutes.
5.  Resuspend pellet in adequate complete medium for reseeding at a subcultivation ratio of 1:4 to 1:2.
6.  Seed cells as described in Section 2.3, Step 13.
7.  Renew medium every 3–4 days as described in Section 2.3, Step 16 until confluence is reached again.

### Protocol Steps

**Protocol for HepG2 Cell Culture and Alginate Bead Encapsulation for Long-Term Culture**

1. **Reagent Preparation (Sterile)**
   1.1. Filter-sterilize all solutions and reagents using 0.22 µm filters (Stericup®/Steritop®, Merck Millipore) and use MiliQ Water (Merck Millipore).
   1.2. Prepare 1.5% (w/v) alginate solution by mixing sodium alginate (216.12 g/mol; viscosity 20,000–40,000 cps, Sigma Aldrich #MKBB8171) with 0.1% (w/v) glucose in PBS.
   1.3. Prepare gelling buffer: 0.1 M calcium chloride (POCH), 0.1% (w/v) glucose, 10 mM HEPES (Sigma Aldrich).
   1.4. Prepare washing buffer: 10 mM calcium chloride, 0.9% (w/v) sodium chloride (Chempur), 0.1% (w/v) glucose, 10 mM HEPES.
   1.5. Oxygenize Perfluorooctylbromide (PFOB) with pure oxygen at 2 mL/min for 1 hour. Connect to encapsulator B-395 Pro (BÜCHI, Switzerland) using a 20 mL syringe with Luer Lock tip.

2. **HepG2 Cell Culture Maintenance**
   2.1. Maintain HepG2 cells in MEM Alpha supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% Glutamine, and 1% Pen-Strep (all Gibco®, Thermo Fisher Scientific) in T-75 culture flasks.
   2.2. Incubate at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂ (Incubator Hera Cell 150, Thermo Scientific).
   2.3. Passage cells weekly using 0.25% Trypsin-EDTA (Invitrogen); resuspend cell pellets in fresh medium.
   2.4. Change medium every 3 days.

3. **Cell Encapsulation Process**
   3.1. After standard passage, divide cells:
       3.1.1. Control cells: seed at 5 × 10⁵ cells/mL on flat surface of a 24-well plate.
       3.1.2. Encapsulation fraction: suspend at 0.5 × 10⁶ cells/mL in 1.5% alginate solution.
   3.2. Use encapsulator B-395 Pro to produce two size groups of structures:
       3.2.1. Smaller structures (diameter ~500 µm) using 200 µm nozzles.
       3.2.2. Larger structures (diameter ~1000 µm) using 400 µm nozzles.
   3.3. Produce microcapsules (MC) and microspheres (MS) using concentric and regular nozzles, respectively:
       3.3.1. For MC: use concentric nozzles (150/200 µm/µm for smaller, 300/400 µm/µm for larger).
       3.3.2. For MS: use regular nozzles (200 µm for smaller, 400 µm for larger).
   3.4. Set droplet formation parameters:
       3.4.1. For 400 µm nozzle: laminar flow 8.0 mL/min, frequency 1000 Hz.
       3.4.2. For 200 µm nozzle: laminar flow 2.2 mL/min, frequency 250 Hz.
   3.5. Configure nozzle connections:
       3.5.1. For MC production: connect PFOB syringe to core nozzle, alginate/cell suspension to shell nozzle. Set PFOB flow rate: 8.0 mL/min for 400 µm nozzle, 2.2 mL/min for 200 µm nozzle.
       3.5.2. For MS production: block the core nozzle.
   3.6. Harden hydrogel droplets by passing them through an electrode for electrostatic charge, then into gelling buffer to avoid aggregation.
   3.7. Remove gelling buffer and wash MC/MS with washing buffer.
   3.8. Place ~250 µL of MC/MS into each well of a 24-well plate.
   3.9. Remove washing buffer and add 1 mL of cell culture medium to each well.
   3.10. Produce control MS and MC without cells using alginate solution lacking cells.

4. **Culture Conditions and Long-Term Maintenance**
   4.1. Place half of the plates in a standard incubator at 37°C, 5% CO₂.
   4.2. Place the remaining half in a hypoxic chamber (STEMCELL Technologies):
       4.2.1. Flush chamber with hypoxic gas mixture (1% O₂/5% CO₂/90% N₂) for 3 hours.
       4.2.2. Hermetically close chamber and place in a 37°C, 5% CO₂ incubator (NuAire).
   4.3. Change medium every 3 days for all plates.
   4.4. Each time the hypoxic chamber is opened:
       4.4.1. Flush it with hypoxic gas mixture using a gas mixer for at least 3 hours.
       4.4.2. Maintain temperature at 37°C during flushing.

5. **Sample Collection and Cell Lysate Preparation**
   5.1. Collect samples on days 1, 2, 7, 14, and 21 from: monolayer cells, MS/MC with cells, and MS/MC without cells.
   5.2. Collect medium samples: remove 500 µL (half of initial volume) per well for glucose and lactate analysis; store at –80°C.
   5.3. Prepare cell lysates:
       5.3.1. Remove remaining medium and wash cells with 1 mL PBS.
       5.3.2. For MS/MC: add 0.5 mL of 0.1 M sodium citrate with 0.1% (w/v) glucose in PBS to dissolve microstructures; incubate at 37°C for 10 minutes.
       5.3.3. For monolayer cells: add 100 µL trypsin-EDTA per well; incubate at 37°C for 5 minutes; add 400 µL fresh medium to neutralize.
       5.3.4. Centrifuge all cell suspensions (200 RCF, 5 minutes) and remove supernatant.
       5.3.5. Resuspend cell residues in 250 µL cold Pierce™ RIPA Buffer; incubate at 4°C for 10 minutes to lyse cells.
       5.3.6. Centrifuge lysate at 14,000 RCF for 15 minutes at 4°C; collect supernatant.
       5.3.7. Store cell lysate supernatant at –80°C for LDH and total protein (BCA) analysis.
